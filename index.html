Research Experience

  • Fluorescence Coupling with Multi-mode and Single-Mode Fiber Array in Ion Trap System

    This is my own independent project. This project aims to couple the fluorescence of a chain of ions to a fiber array. Initially, I simulated the ion array fluorescence using a pinhole array and built an optical system consisting of a pinhole array, objective lens, tube lens, and fiber array. I first imaged the pinhole array on the camera and calculated the magnification of the optical imaging system. Then, I focused on coupling the fluorescence to a multimode fiber array and evaluating its efficiency.

    In this project, I developed skills in optical path construction and adjustment. I gained the ability to independently construct experimental platforms. I also learned how to realize hardware-software integration.

  • Construction of Optical Paths for Quantum Computation Relay System Based on Ion Trap

    In November, 2023, I have been part of a research group focused on Quantum Communication Relays using Ion Traps, under the supervision of Prof. Yong Wan. This group is part of the Department of Quantum Physics and Quantum Information, with Professor Jianwei Pan, an academician, serving as the director.

    I was responsible for assisting my senior colleagues in constructing optical paths. I started with fiber coupling, and I am now proficient in it. I have also constructed the PDH frequency locking optical path, optical paths for laser cooling, optical paths for measuring frequency noise, and many other optical paths for the ion trap quantum system. At this stage, I acquired essential knowledge, including the principles of the PDH method, AOM, and other fundamental concepts. I also developed key skills and techniques for quantum experiments.
